A high-performance framework for structured matrices

sTiles targets the full Cholesky → solve → selected inverse pipeline on a single shared-memory node, using tile-based parallelism.

Today’s release handles symmetric positive-definite matrices across the entire spectrum, from very sparse to fully dense, with one unified solver.

Distributed-memory support is on the roadmap.

When you take a side: dense vs. sparse
1

Sparse matrix, one solve: use the native sparse solver your platform already ships. A one-off solve never repays a heavy analysis.

“Don't use a sledgehammer to crack a nut.”
2

Don't use a sparse solver on a dense matrix. Once the pattern is nearly full, the indexing costs more than the sparsity saves.

“You can't get blood from a stone.”
3

Small sparse matrix: a dense solver is fine. Memory will be happy either way.

“Don't sweat the small stuff.”
4

Know your sparsity pattern. No fixed mode wins everywhere, and which one is right changes with the regime.

“Dense matrices are all alike; every sparse matrix is sparse in its own way.”
When you take a side: dense vs. sparse
5

Order matters. The wrong ordering turns a sparse matrix into a dense factor.

“Don't put the cart before the horse.”
6

Speed is satisfying, but efficiency is what scales. We can be slower on the small matrices and faster on the ones that cost real time.

“Lose the battle, win the war.”
7

Invest once, reuse a thousand times. The analysis is the heaviest of any solver, but it pays for itself in a handful of factorizations, and the same pattern is factorized again and again.

“Measure twice, cut once.”
8

Not every matrix belongs on a GPU. The A100 is at parity on the two smallest dense matrices and 6.3× faster on the largest.

“Horses for courses.”
The best side is not to take one. Let the matrix decide.
